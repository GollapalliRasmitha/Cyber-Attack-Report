What are Deepfakes?

🎬

AI Video Synthesis

Deepfakes use Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to swap faces or manipulate expressions in video with hyper-realistic changes.

🔊

Voice Cloning

AI can analyze a few seconds of audio to clone a person's voice, allowing scammers to impersonate loved ones or CEOs.

🆔

Synthetic Identity

Entirely new "people" can be generated who never existed, often used for fake profiles and social engineering bots.

Detection Guide: Spot the Glitch

1. Note the Eyes

Look for unnatural blinking patterns (too fast/slow) or shadows that don't match the scene lighting.

2. Lip Sync & Texture

Audio-visual lag is common. Also, check for blurring inside the mouth or missing teeth details.

3. Face Edges

Blurry hairlines or flickering at the edge of the face where the AI mask meets the original video.

4. Skin Tone

Inconsistent skin tone between the face and the neck/body is a major red flag.

ANALYZING MEDIA...

Proof of Personhood

In an era of AI, verifying humanity is crucial.

Digital Watermarking

Invisible cryptographic signatures embedded in media at the point of capture (C2PA standards) to prove authenticity.

Liveness Detection

Challenge-response tests (e.g., "turn your head left") used by banking apps to ensure a real user is present.

Blockchain Identity

Decentralized identifiers (DIDs) that allow individuals to own their digital likeness and verify content they publish.

Prevention & Awareness

  • Don't Trust Caller ID: Numbers can be spoofed. Hang up and call back on a verified line.
  • Verify Requests: If a "relative" asks for money via voice note, verify with a secret code word.
  • Check Metadata: Use tools that read C2PA/IPTC metadata to check image history.
  • Limit Public Data: High-res photos and voice samples online are training data for AI.
